## 🔍 IV. Phân Tích Chuyên Sâu Các Từ Vựng, Cụm Từ & Thành Ngữ Trọng Điểm (Detailed Lexicon Analysis - A2 đến C1+)

### 1. **[[Pastime]]** /ˈpɑːs.taɪm/ & **[[Avocation]]** /ˌæv.əˈkeɪ.ʃən/ *(Nouns - B2 đến C2)*
* **Định nghĩa song ngữ (Bilingual Definition):**
  - **EN:** *Pastime:* An activity that someone does regularly for enjoyment rather than work. *Avocation:* A hobby or minor occupation pursued with passion alongside one's primary profession.
  - **VN:** *Pastime:* Thú tiêu khiển, trò giải trí lúc nhàn rỗi. *Avocation:* Đam mê ngoài giờ, nghề tay trái xuất phát từ sở thích.
* **Giải thích chi tiết Sắc thái & Ngữ cảnh (Nuance, Usage & Synonym Comparison):**
  - *Tránh lặp từ `[[Hobby]]`:* Trong bài thi IELTS Speaking, nếu đề hỏi *"What are your hobbies?"*, việc lặp lại từ `hobby` 5-6 lần sẽ làm giảm điểm Lexical Resource. Hãy luân phiên dùng **`pastime`** (B2), **`recreational pursuit`** (B2/C1), và **`avocation`** (C2 - khi sở thích đó nghiêm túc như một sự nghiệp thứ hai, đối lập với *vocation* là nghề chính).
* **Cụm từ đi kèm (Collocations):**
  - *a beloved national pastime* (một thú tiêu khiển được cả dân tộc yêu thích)
  - *a sedentary / solitary pastime* (một thú vui ít vận động / thú vui một mình)
  - *pursue photography as a serious avocation* (theo đuổi nhiếp ảnh như một đam mê nghiêm túc ngoài giờ)
* **Ví dụ cụ thể (Concrete Examples):**

### 12. **Have a knack for (something)** /hæv ə næk fɔːr/ & **Cut out for** /kʌt aʊt fɔːr/ *(Idioms - B2/C1)*
* **Định nghĩa song ngữ (Bilingual Definition):**
  - **EN:** *Have a knack for:* To have an innate talent or natural skill at doing something easily. *Be cut out for:* To naturally have the right qualities and temperament for a particular activity or role.
  - **VN:** *Have a knack for:* Có năng khiếu bẩm sinh, có "hoa tay" làm việc gì. *Be cut out for:* Sinh ra để làm việc gì, có tố chất phù hợp với việc gì.
* **Giải thích chi tiết Sắc thái & Ngữ cảnh (Nuance, Usage & Synonym Comparison):**
  - *Mẹo dùng đối lập:* Dùng **`have a natural knack for + V-ing/Noun`** khi khen mình (hoặc người khác) khéo tay, có khiếu. Dùng dạng phủ định **`not be cut out for + V-ing/Noun`** khi kể về một sở thích bạn đã thử nhưng thất bại vì không hợp tố chất.
* **Cụm từ đi kèm (Collocations):**
  - *have a natural knack for handicrafts / languages* (có năng khiếu bẩm sinh về làm đồ thủ công / ngôn ngữ)
  - *not be cut out for extreme sports* (không có tố chất phù hợp với thể thao mạo hiểm)
* **Ví dụ cụ thể (Concrete Examples):**
  - *IELTS Speaking Part 1:* "My mother **has a natural knack for** [[Flower arrangement]]; she can turn simple wildflowers into a stunning centerpiece." *(Mẹ tôi có năng khiếu bẩm sinh về cắm hoa; bà có thể biến những bông hoa dại giản dị thành một bình hoa trưng bày tuyệt đẹp.)*
  - *IELTS Speaking Part 2:* "After spraining my ankle twice, I realized I simply wasn't **cut out for** [[Skateboarding]] and switched to [[Swimming]] instead." *(Sau hai lần bị bong gân mắt cá chân, tôi nhận ra mình đơn giản là không có tố chất hợp với môn trượt ván và đã chuyển sang bơi lội.)*

## 🎙️ VIII. Part 3: Câu Hỏi Thảo Luận Mở Rộng (Discussion Questions)

### Q1: Is it important for individuals to have hobbies separate from their professional work?

#### 🟢 Level 1 [A2-B1] (Band 5.0 - 5.5):
> *"Yes, it is very important. If people only work and have no hobbies, they will feel very tired and sad. Having a hobby helps us relax on the weekend, forget work problems, and come back to the office happier on Monday."*

#### 🟡 Level 2 [B2] (Band 6.0 - 6.5):
> *"Yes, it is extremely vital. Without hobbies, people will easily become workaholics and get burned out. Having a hobby gives you something enjoyable to look forward to after a long, exhausting week."*

#### 🔵 Level 3 [C1+] (Band 7.5 - 8.5):
> *"I consider cultivating extracurricular passions an absolute necessity rather than an optional indulgence. Dedicated pastimes act as an indispensable **psychological buffer** against occupational distress. When individuals anchor their entire self-worth exclusively to their professional achievements, any career setback can trigger acute existential crises. Engaging in a non-monetized hobby fosters identity diversification and restores mental equilibrium."*

---

### Q2: Why do some people turn their hobbies into a career, and is that always a good idea?

#### 🟢 Level 1 [A2-B1] (Band 5.0 - 5.5):
> *"Many people turn their hobby into a job because they want to make money doing what they love, like baking cakes or taking photos. However, it is not always good because when you have to please customers and worry about money, the hobby becomes stressful and stops being fun."*

#### 🟡 Level 2 [B2] (Band 6.0 - 6.5):
> *"The main appeal of monetizing a hobby is the dream of never feeling like you're working a day in your life. However, it can be a double-edged sword. Once a creative pastime is tied to client deadlines, financial pressure, and market algorithms, the original joy often evaporates, leaving you without a way to relax."*

#### 🔵 Level 3 [C1+] (Band 7.5 - 8.5):
> *"While commercializing an **avocation** is romanticized by modern hustle culture, it frequently triggers what psychologists term the **'overjustification effect'**. When an intrinsically rewarding pursuit is subjected to extrinsic financial imperatives, client whims, and profit margins, the purity of creative expression is inevitably compromised. Consequently, transforming a sanctuary of escapism into a livelihood can inadvertently strip the activity of its **cathartic value**."*

---

### Q3: How has technology changed the way people spend their leisure time compared to 50 years ago?

#### 🟢 Level 1 [A2-B1] (Band 5.0 - 5.5):
> *"Fifty years ago, people spent their free time outside talking to neighbors, playing sports, or reading newspapers. Today, because of smartphones and the internet, most people stay indoors to watch Netflix, play online games, or scroll TikTok alone."*

#### 🟡 Level 2 [B2] (Band 6.0 - 6.5):
> *"Technology has shifted leisure from active, community-based gatherings to passive, screen-centered consumption. Half a century ago, people had to go out to cinemas, parks, or clubs to entertain themselves. Nowadays, streaming platforms and social media deliver endless entertainment straight to our couches, which is convenient but makes people much more sedentary and isolated."*

#### 🔵 Level 3 [C1+] (Band 7.5 - 8.5):
> *"The digital revolution has precipitated a paradigm shift from **communal, embodied recreation to atomized, sedentary consumption**. Fifty years ago, leisure was predominantly physical and geographically anchored in third places—neighborhood parks, civic halls, and theaters. Today, algorithmic streaming ecosystems and virtual gaming worlds provide frictionless, on-demand entertainment, which, while democratizing access to global culture, has exacerbated **physical inertia and social fragmentation**."*

### Q4: Why are some people drawn to extreme or dangerous sports like skydiving or rock climbing?

#### 🟢 Level 1 [A2-B1] (Band 5.0 - 5.5):
> *"I think some people love dangerous sports because their normal life is too boring and safe. When they jump from a plane or climb a high mountain, they feel very excited, strong, and proud of themselves."*

#### 🟡 Level 2 [B2] (Band 6.0 - 6.5):
> *"People who gravitate toward extreme sports are usually thrill-seekers chasing an adrenaline rush. Modern office life can feel very monotonous and predictable, so engaging in high-risk activities forces them to be 100% present in the moment and gives them an incredible sense of triumph when they conquer their fears."*

#### 🔵 Level 3 [C1+] (Band 7.5 - 8.5):
> *"The allure of high-stakes, **adrenaline-pumping pursuits** stems from a primal psychological craving for intensity in an otherwise sanitized, risk-averse modern existence. Confronting visceral physical danger triggers a surge of endorphins and dopamine while compelling the practitioner into a state of absolute **cognitive flow**. Overcoming mortal apprehension on a sheer cliff face instills a profound sense of self-efficacy and existential aliveness."*

### Q5: Do you think modern people have less free time than in the past, or do they just waste more time?

#### 🟢 Level 1 [A2-B1] (Band 5.0 - 5.5):
> *"I think people actually have enough free time, but they waste too much of it on smartphones. Many people say they have no time to exercise or read books, but they spend three hours every night watching short videos on social media."*

#### 🟡 Level 2 [B2] (Band 6.0 - 6.5):
> *"In my opinion, it's mostly a perception problem caused by digital distractions. Statistics show that working hours haven't increased drastically for most people, yet we feel constantly time-poor because our spare hours are fragmented and swallowed up by mindless scrolling on smartphones."*

#### 🔵 Level 3 [C1+] (Band 7.5 - 8.5):
> *"While economic pressures certainly compel some demographics to work longer hours, for the vast majority of white-collar workers, perceived time scarcity is an illusion engineered by the **attention economy**. We do not necessarily possess less raw leisure time than previous generations; rather, our unencumbered hours are systematically hijacked and fragmented by addictive digital algorithms. Consequently, two hours evaporated in a dopamine-induced scrolling trance leaves us feeling cognitively depleted rather than genuinely rested."*
